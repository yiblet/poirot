# Brevity

Keep material that earns its place in helping the reader understand or use the document.
Apply that standard recursively: a useful document contains useful sections,
each section contains useful paragraphs, and each paragraph contains useful
sentences. Concise wording does not rescue unnecessary content.

## Establish what the whole document must deliver

Identify the reader, their task, and what the document should enable them to
understand, decide, or do. Check whether this document is needed at all: an
existing document may already serve the same purpose better. Use the intended
outcome to judge the content, rather than treating everything already written
as something to preserve.

For an editing request, revise within the authorized scope. For a review, report
findings. If the user asks for a paragraph-by-paragraph analysis, describe each
paragraph's purpose, what the reader is scanning for there, what it actually
delivers, and the resulting decision. Keep that analysis separate from the
reader-facing document.

## Review from the whole down to each sentence

At each level, ask what this part contributes to its enclosing section or
paragraph. Identify what the reader needs at this point and whether the text
delivers it directly. Apply the same reasoning to list items, table rows,
examples, notes, and captions rather than assuming their format earns them a place.

For every part, consider:

- What would the reader lose if it were removed: understanding, orientation,
  useful interest, or help with a decision or action? If nothing useful is lost,
  remove it.
- Does it belong here? An explanation of why, a useful connection, or an answer
  to a likely reader question can earn its place beyond the immediate task. Move
  detail serving a different purpose to the appropriate place; remove irrelevant
  material rather than finding somewhere else to put it.
- Does a sibling paragraph, section, or existing guide already explain this
  better? Keep the explanation where it best serves the reader, consolidating
  or linking when that avoids duplication without forcing needless navigation.
- Does it deliver its purpose plainly, or bury the answer in qualifications,
  terminology, and secondary goals? Rewrite around the answer.

Do this recursively. A necessary section can contain unnecessary paragraphs, and
an otherwise useful paragraph can contain sentences that contribute nothing.
Removing one passage may also make its introduction, qualifications, examples,
or cross-references unnecessary; reassess them together.

## Give each paragraph a clear job

Make the paragraph directly satisfy the reader's need at that point. Keep only
sentences that establish, explain, support, or qualify that point in a way the
reader needs. Allow at most one or two sentences serving a wider purpose, and
only when that context helps the reader understand or use this paragraph.

A sentence that requires an unrelated explanation may belong elsewhere. A
paragraph with several independent jobs may need splitting, while adjacent
paragraphs repeating the same job may need combining. Make those choices from
the meaning, not from a fixed length or sentence count.

## Preserve what makes the explanation useful

A useful explanation can satisfy curiosity and anticipate what a reader would
naturally ask next. Keep reasons, revealing categories, examples, and connections
when they build understanding. For instance, grouping unsupported functions by
what they do can explain a library's limits better than a shorter list of names.
Judge each contribution; do not remove all context or turn every paragraph into
bare instructions.

Keep room for personality, humor, vivid examples, and varied sentence structure.
Style can make an idea clearer, more engaging, or more memorable; those are
valuable contributions too. Preserve effective phrasing and rhythm rather than
flattening prose into short, uniform sentences or a mechanical template.

Keep necessary definitions, evidence, examples, constraints, failure behavior,
and attribution. Brevity should not make a claim broader, less accurate, or
harder to understand. Some repetition is useful for a summary, comparison, or
independently read reference entry; retain it when it serves that distinct use.

Prefer established vocabulary and concrete behavior. Do not compress an
explanation into a new label that merely hides complexity. Clarify uncertain
meaning before shortening it.

## Read the result as a whole

After editing the parts, reread the complete document in order. Confirm that it
still answers the reader's questions, introduces necessary ideas before using
them, and has no gaps, contradictions, orphaned references, or repeated jobs.
Then check the surviving paragraphs and sentences again against their purposes.

Explain review findings through the contribution that is missing, misplaced, or
duplicated and its effect on the reader. Do not justify removal merely by word
count or call a passage pointless without showing why its purpose is unnecessary
or better served elsewhere.
